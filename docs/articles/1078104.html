The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse in Industrial ML

When we at Data Sapience began delving into predictive analytics tasks for the oil and gas industry, we encountered fundamental problems that repeated from plant to plant, from company to company.

The First Horseman – Pestilence (symptoms of the disease spread across sources)
Data from ACS TP sensors live in one world (OPC servers, SCADA), operational MES tasks are in another, and economic indicators from ERP are in a third. There is no unified picture. A process engineer cannot see how raw material quality affects pump vibration after three hours of operation. A mechanic doesn't understand that a pressure change in one circuit signals problems in a neighboring one. Everyone looks at their fragment of the puzzle.

At one facility, we counted 149 tags related to a single compressor. But they were scattered across different systems. One part was in the PI System, another in SCADA, and a third in Excel spreadsheets held by process engineers. Some tags had strange names that only one engineer, who left last year, understood. And some tags weren't collected at all: they simply weren't fed into a unified system.

The Second Horseman – War (with accidents)
Production culture is often built on the principle of "firefighting." A breakdown occurs – repair crews are immediately called. Off-spec product is released – we deal with it after the fact. Unit downtime can cost hundreds of thousands of rubles per hour. But instead of preventing incidents, there's only chronic elimination of consequences. The problem here is not an unwillingness to prevent accidents, but a lack of tools that would show in advance: "Listen, your pump will fail in 60 days. The cause is in these seals. Deal with it now, and everything will be fine."
